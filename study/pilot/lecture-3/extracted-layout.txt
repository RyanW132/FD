 Why Do Interest Rates Matter?

  Interest rates enter almost every derivatives-pricing problem because cash
  flows occurring at different dates cannot be compared directly.
          They determine how future cash flows are discounted.
          They affect bond prices, forward prices, swaps, options, and other
          derivatives.
          They influence borrowing, saving, investment, exchange rates, and
          monetary policy.
          Different rates reflect both the value of time and, in many cases,
          credit, liquidity, and collateral risk.

  Central question
  How much is a payment at a future date worth today?

 Interest Rates Are Not All the Same
  Government bond rates Yields on debt issued by governments; these vary
                by currency, maturity, liquidity, and sovereign credit risk.
   Policy rates Rates set or targeted by central banks as part of monetary
                policy.
    Repo rates Rates on secured borrowing in which securities serve as
                collateral.
  Reference rates Transaction-based benchmarks used in loans and
                derivatives, such as SONIA for sterling and SOFR for US
                dollars.
  Risk-free rate
  The risk-free rate is a modelling ideal: the return on an investment with
  no default risk. In modern derivatives markets, overnight-indexed rates are
  often used to construct discount curves, subject to the contract and
  collateral arrangements.

 What Does ”10% Per Annum” Mean?


  The quotation is incomplete unless its compounding convention is stated.
  One-year investment
  Invest $100 at a nominal annual rate of 10%.
          Annual compounding: 100 × 1.10 = $110.00
          Semiannual compounding: 100(1 + 0.10/2)2 = $110.25
          Continuous compounding: 100e 0.10 = $110.52

  The quoted rate alone is therefore not enough to determine the terminal
  value.

 Zero Rates and Discount Factors

  The T -year zero rate is the return on an investment that makes no
  intermediate payments and pays only at time T .
  With a continuously compounded zero rate R(T ), the discount factor is

                                            P(0, T ) = e −R(T )T .

  The present value of a cash flow CT is

                                PV (CT ) = CT P(0, T ) = CT e −R(T )T .

  Example
  If the five-year zero rate is 5%, then

                      100e 0.05×5 = $128.40,                   100e −0.05×5 = $77.88.

 What Is Bootstrapping?


  Zero rates can be inferred from prices of traded instruments.
          A zero-coupon instrument reveals its maturity’s discount factor
          directly.
          A coupon bond contains several cash flows.
          Once earlier discount factors are known, subtract the present value of
          the earlier coupons and solve for the next discount factor.
          Repeat sequentially along the maturity structure.

  Why the name?
  Each newly determined zero rate is used to obtain the next one.

 What Is a Forward Rate Agreement?

  A forward rate agreement (FRA) is an over-the-counter contract that fixes
  the interest rate applied to a notional principal during a future period
  [T1 , T2 ].
          The principal is normally not exchanged.
          The parties exchange only an interest-rate difference.
          One party receives fixed and pays the future market reference rate.
          The other party pays fixed and receives the market rate.
          Settlement normally occurs at T1 , so the T2 difference is discounted
          back to T1 .

  Economic purpose
  A borrower can use an FRA to protect against rising rates; a lender can
  use one to protect against falling rates.

 Which Side of the FRA?

 Duration, Convexity, and Immunisation


  Matching the duration of assets and liabilities can reduce sensitivity to
  small parallel yield-curve shifts.
          Convexity improves protection against larger parallel shifts.
          Duration and convexity change as time passes and yields move.
          Matching them does not eliminate exposure to non-parallel curve
          movements.
          Credit spreads, liquidity, optionality, and model risk may remain.

  Risk-management lesson
  Duration and convexity describe particular dimensions of interest-rate risk;
  they do not make a portfolio riskless.

 Lecture Summary
          Interest-rate quotations require a compounding convention.
          Zero rates discount individual cash flows at their respective maturities.
          Bond yield is a single internal rate; par yield is the coupon rate that
          makes a bond trade at par.
          Bootstrapping extracts discount factors sequentially from market
          prices.
          Forward rates are implied by today’s zero curve.
          FRAs fix a rate for a future borrowing or lending period.
          Duration measures first-order price sensitivity; convexity provides a
          second-order correction.
  Unifying idea
