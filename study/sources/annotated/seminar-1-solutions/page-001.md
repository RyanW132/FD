Give an example of a hedger, a speculator, and an arbitrageur in derivatives markets.

## Supplied solution: hedger

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: hedger.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

A wheat farmer expects to sell wheat in three months and is concerned that its price may fall. The farmer shorts wheat futures. If wheat prices fall, the gain on the futures position offsets some or all of the lower cash-market revenue. The hedge stabilises the selling price, although basis risk may remain.

## Supplied solution: speculator

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: speculator.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

A trader expects crude-oil prices to rise and buys oil futures without having an existing oil exposure. The trader profits if the futures price rises and loses if it falls. The objective is to take risk in anticipation of a return.

## Supplied solution: arbitrageur

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: arbitrageur.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-1-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

Suppose gold is priced at $2,000 per ounce and carrying costs imply a fair one-year futures price of $2,120, but the market futures price is $2,150. The arbitrageur can buy gold, finance and carry it for one year, and short the futures contract. At maturity, the gold is delivered for $2,150, generating an arbitrage profit of

$$2150-2120=30$$

 dollars per ounce, before transaction costs.
