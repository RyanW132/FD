Because the farmer will sell wheat in three months and is concerned that its price may fall, the farmer should take a short futures hedge.

$$N=\frac{\text{quantity hedged}}{\text{contract size}}=\frac{50{,}000}{5{,}000}=10\text{ short contracts}.$$

## Supplied solution: spot revenue, futures gain and effective price

**Physical page 4; source region: Supplied solution: spot revenue, futures gain and effective price.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 4](../../assets/seminar-1-solutions/page-004.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-1-solutions/page-004.md#page-4)

At harvest, the farmer sells the physical wheat in the spot market:

$$\text{Spot revenue}=50{,}000(4.80)=240{,}000\text{ dollars}.$$

The short futures position gains $5.20-4.85=0.35$ dollars per bushel. Therefore,

$$\text{Futures gain}=50{,}000(0.35)=17{,}500\text{ dollars}.$$

Total proceeds are $240{,}000+17{,}500=257{,}500$ dollars. Hence,

$$\text{Effective price}=\frac{257{,}500}{50{,}000}=5.15\text{ dollars per bushel}.$$

Equivalently,

$$\text{Effective price}=S_T+(F_0-F_T)=4.80+(5.20-4.85)=5.15.$$

## Supplied solution: imperfect hedge and closing basis

**Physical page 4; source region: Supplied solution: imperfect hedge and closing basis.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 4](../../assets/seminar-1-solutions/page-004.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-1-solutions/page-004.md#page-4)

The hedge is imperfect because of basis risk. The closing basis is

$$b_T=S_T-F_T=4.80-4.85=-0.05\text{ dollars}.$$

Thus,

$$\text{Effective price}=F_0+b_T=5.20-0.05=5.15\text{ dollars}.$$

The effective price differs from the initial futures price because spot and futures prices do not move perfectly together. Differences in quality, location, timing, transaction costs, and the quantity actually harvested may also reduce hedge effectiveness.
