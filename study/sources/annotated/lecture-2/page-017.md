## Hedge ratio symbols

**Source region:** upper formula and symbol definitions. **Classification:** substantive.

In

$$h^*=\rho\frac{\sigma_S}{\sigma_F},$$

$\sigma_S$ is the standard deviation of spot-price **changes**, $\sigma_F$ that of futures-price **changes**, and $\rho$ their correlation.

## Hedge ratio interpretation

**Source region:** central three interpretation bullets. **Classification:** substantive.

- A higher correlation generally makes the hedge more effective.
- $h^*$ need not equal one.
- The hedge minimises variance; it does not necessarily eliminate risk.

**Scope note (author annotation):** “higher correlation” is the source's wording. A general signed-correlation treatment must distinguish correlation magnitude and hedge direction; this annotation does not silently broaden the source claim to all negative correlations.

## Regression hedge

**Source region:** lower regression-interpretation box. **Classification:** substantive.

In the regression

$$\Delta S_t=\alpha+h\Delta F_t+\varepsilon_t,$$

the estimated slope is the minimum-variance hedge ratio. $\alpha$ denotes the intercept and $\varepsilon_t$ the regression residual (notation clarification); the regressor is the futures **price change**, not its price level.
