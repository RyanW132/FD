## Target beta formula

**Source region:** upper target-beta statement and boxed formula. **Classification:** substantive.

To change portfolio beta from $\beta_P$ to target $\beta^*$, use

$$N^*=(\beta_P-\beta^*)\frac{V_A}{V_F}.$$

$V_A$ is portfolio value and $V_F$ the notional value of one futures contract, in matching currency units. The full beta difference multiplies the entire value ratio.

**Sign-convention clarification (author annotation):** $N^*$ is signed in the direction of contracts to short: a positive value means short that many contracts, while a negative value means buy its magnitude.

## Target beta direction

**Source region:** central direction and zero-target bullets. **Classification:** substantive.

- If $\beta_P>\beta^*$, **short** futures.
- If $\beta_P<\beta^*$, **buy** futures.
- Setting $\beta^*=0$ removes the **estimated market exposure**.

**Sign-convention clarification (author annotation):** the displayed expression is signed in the direction of contracts to short; a negative result corresponds to buying its magnitude. It is not a negative physical contract count. Removing estimated market exposure does not remove all portfolio risk.

## Beta neutral special case

**Source region:** lower special-case box. **Classification:** substantive.

For a beta-neutral hedge:

$$N^*=\beta_P\frac{V_A}{V_F}.$$

**Author clarification:** this uses the same signed-to-short convention: a positive $N^*$ means short that many contracts; a negative value means buy its magnitude. The hedge removes estimated systematic market exposure under the model, not all portfolio risk.
