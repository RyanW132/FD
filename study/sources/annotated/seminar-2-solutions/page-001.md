## Supplied solution: standardization

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: standardization.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

Forwards are private, customizable agreements between two parties. Futures are standardized contracts traded on organized exchanges. Options are standardized when exchange-traded but can be customized in OTC markets.

<a id="seminar-2-solutions-p001-q1-settlement"></a>

## Supplied solution: settlement

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: settlement.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

Forwards are typically settled at maturity by physical delivery or cash settlement. Futures are marked to market daily, with gains and losses settled each day through a clearing house. For exchange-traded options, the clearinghouse substantially reduces counterparty risk. For OTC options, the buyer may suffer a loss if the option writer defaults. The buyer’s maximum loss from movements in the underlying price is normally the premium paid.

<a id="seminar-2-solutions-p001-q1-credit-risk"></a>

## Supplied solution: credit risk — conflicting source wording retained

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: credit risk — conflicting source wording retained.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

The source states:

“Forwards carry significant counterparty risk because they are private contracts without an intermediary. Futures have minimal credit risk since the exchange clearing house guarantees performance. Option buyers face no default risk beyond the premium paid, while writers have potential exposure unless collateralized.”

**Source qualification:** the option-buyer default statement conflicts with the Settlement paragraph on this same page, which correctly acknowledges loss when an OTC option writer defaults. Premium-limited market loss is not immunity from counterparty default; preserve the contradiction rather than teach the quoted assertion as a general rule. Clearing/collateral reduces and manages counterparty risk, not every possible default or operational risk.

## Supplied solution: typical market participants

**Physical page 1; source region: Supplied solution: typical market participants.**

![Original full uncropped physical page 1](../../assets/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.png)

[Preserved raw extraction](../../extracted/seminar-2-solutions/page-001.md#page-1)

Forwards are used mainly by corporations and financial institutions for tailored hedging needs. Futures attract hedgers and speculators requiring liquidity and transparency. Options are widely used by hedgers, speculators, and portfolio managers seeking asymmetric payoff structures.
