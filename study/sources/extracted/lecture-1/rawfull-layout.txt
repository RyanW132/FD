 What Do These Contracts Have in Common?


  Consider three contracts
      A contract fixing the price of oil delivered in three months
          A contract paying £100 if the FTSE 100 exceeds a specified level
          A contract paying a farmer if rainfall falls below a threshold

  Question: What determines the value of each contract?
  Key idea
  A derivative is a financial contract whose value depends on the value or
  outcome of an underlying asset, rate, index, or event.

  Examples include forwards, futures, swaps, and options.

 Before 2008: Risk Was Difficult to See

  A network of bilateral promises
  Many OTC derivatives were negotiated privately between banks and other
  institutions.

          Contracts could be tailored to the parties’ needs
          Banks commonly acted as dealers and market makers
          Counterparty exposures accumulated across institutions
          Reporting and price transparency were limited
          Many contracts were not centrally cleared

  The systemic-risk problem
  If one major counterparty failed, losses could spread through a network of
  contracts whose total exposure was difficult for participants and regulators
  to observe.

 After the Crisis: Making OTC Markets Safer

  The post-crisis reforms
  Depending on the contract, counterparty, and jurisdiction:
          eligible standardised OTC derivatives may have to be cleared through
          a central counterparty;
          derivative transactions are reported to trade repositories;
          uncleared contracts may face margin and risk-management rules;
          some standardised contracts trade on organised platforms.

  Quick check
  Which reform primarily addresses each problem?
      1   Regulators cannot observe accumulated exposures.
      2   One counterparty may fail before paying what it owes.
      3   A customised contract cannot be centrally cleared.

 What Does a Central Counterparty Do?

  Without central clearing
  Bank A and Bank B are directly exposed to each other’s default.

  With central clearing
  The CCP becomes:
          the buyer to Bank A’s sale; and
          the seller to Bank B’s purchase.

  Key implication
  The original trade remains economically matched, but counterparty
  exposure is managed through the CCP’s margin and default procedures.

  Question: Does clearing eliminate risk or reorganise and manage it?

 The Long Forward Payoff Is ST − K


  Suppose the delivery price is K = 105.

 A Futures Contract Resembles a Forward - But Trades
 Differently
  What remains the same?
  A futures contract creates an obligation to buy or sell an underlying asset
  at a specified price and future date.

                                             Forward                                Futures
          Trading venue                    Usually OTC                         Exchange-traded
          Contract terms                   Customised                            Standardised
          Counterparty                     Other party                           Clearinghouse
          Settlement                    Mainly at maturity                   Daily gains and losses
          Margin                        Contract-dependent                  Margin account required

  Key question
  If long and short futures payoffs offset each other, why does the exchange
  require both parties to post margin?

 Futures Gains and Losses Are Settled Daily
  Example
  A trader takes a long position in one futures contract.
  Each contract covers 100 units, and the futures price rises from £50 to
  £53 during the day.

  Daily gain

                              Daily gain = contract size × price change
                                            = 100(53 − 50) = 300.

  General result
  For a long position in N contracts, each covering q units,

                                            ∆Vt = Nq(Ft − Ft−1 ).

  For a short position, the sign is reversed.

 Margin Reduces the Risk of Non-Payment
          Initial margin: funds deposited when the position is opened
          Variation margin: daily gains or losses added to the account
          Maintenance margin: minimum permitted account balance
          Margin call: request to restore the account balance

  Calculate the margin balance
  A trader deposits initial margin of £2,000.
  The futures position loses £600 on Day 1 and £500 on Day 2.

                                     Margin balance after Day 2 = ?

  Answer
                                            2,000 − 600 − 500 = 900.
  If the maintenance margin exceeds £900, the trader receives a margin call.

 Would You Pay to Keep a Choice Open?


  Two contracts
  You may acquire an asset in three months for £100.
      1   You are obliged to buy it for £100.
      2   You have the right, but not the obligation, to buy it for £100.

  The key distinction
      A forward or futures contract creates an obligation.
          An option gives its buyer a right.
          The option buyer pays a premium for that right.

  Question: Which contract is more valuable to its holder and why?

 Payoff Is Not the Same as Profit
  Long-call payoff

                                            PayoffT = max(ST − K , 0).

  Long-call profit
  Ignoring interest on the initial premium,

                                     ProfitT = max(ST − K , 0) − c0 ,

  where c0 is the option premium.

  Break-even
  For a call with K = 100 and premium c0 = 8, at what underlying price
  does the buyer break even?


                                               ST = K + c0 = 108.

 When May the Option Be Exercised?


  European option
  May be exercised only on its expiration date.

  American option
  May be exercised at any time up to and including its expiration date.

  Concept check
  An American option is otherwise identical to a European option.
  Should the American option ever be worth less?

  No. It provides at least the same exercise opportunities and possibly more.
